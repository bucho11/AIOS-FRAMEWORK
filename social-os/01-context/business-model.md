## The ownership question (his Q10)

> "i do want her to own it but i should right? since i will be reselling it?"

**Both, split by asset class.** Holding the wrong thing creates a hostage
situation; holding nothing means you cannot resell. The standard agency split:

| Asset | Owner | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta assets** (IG, FB Page) | **Client, always** | Never hold a client's social accounts. If the relationship ends and you hold them, you are the villain and possibly liable. Non-negotiable. |
| **Google Drive folder** | **Client owns; you are Editor** | It is her brand, her content, her data. If she leaves she keeps it — that is a *feature* you sell, not a loss. Holding her data hostage is how referrals die, and referral to the brand owner is the whole play. |
| **Composio account** | **You** | One account, all clients as connected accounts. Confirmed his preference. |
| **Publisher account** (Ayrshare/etc) | **You** | Per-profile pricing only works if you hold the account. This is your margin. |
| **Image account** (Bannerbear/etc) | **You** | One account serves every client. Pure operator infrastructure. |
| **Skills / templates / the OS itself** | **You** | This is the IP. It is what you resell. |

**The line:** *she owns her data and her audience; you own the machine.* She can
walk away with everything that is hers and still have nothing that competes with
you. That is what makes it safe to sell her.

**Practical setup:** create the folder in **her** Google account, add you as
Editor. If she has no Google account, create one **for her business** with her as
owner — not a folder inside your personal Drive.

**The shape that matters:** client #1 is expensive, clients 2–20 are cheap. Do not
price client #1 off its own cost — price it off the portfolio, or you will
underprice the thing you actually want, which is the brand-wide deal.

## Two sales, not one

1. **The franchisee sale** — her location. Proves it works. Low price is fine.
2. **The brand sale** — Carson Wagner / corporate, ~20 markets. This is the real
   revenue, and client #1 is the demo.

Build #1 as a *reference implementation*, not a bespoke job. Every hour spent
making it clonable is an hour spent on sale #2.
