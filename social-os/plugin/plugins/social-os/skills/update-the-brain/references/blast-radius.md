# Blast radius — what a change breaks, and fixing it in the same turn

A rule document is not a note. It is the input to everything already built from it.
The moment `Brand voice` changes, every draft written in the old voice is wrong —
and nobody sees the problem, because the drafts still look fine.

**Handle the fallout before you tell her you are done.** A correction that leaves
stale work behind is the correction coming back in a week, and by then she is
correcting the same thing twice, which is the repeat signal — the most serious one
there is.

---

## The table

Fixed dependencies, knowable in advance. Check the row, act on it.

| When this changes | Re-check and deal with |
|---|---|
| **Brand voice** | every unpublished draft in `2 Drafts/`; anything in `3 Approved/` not yet out; the current week's plan; any graphic with words on it |
| **Who we talk to** | unpublished drafts; the week's plan; the pillar mix — a change of audience usually changes *what* to post, not just how it reads |
| **What we offer** | unpublished drafts; anything approved-but-unsent; any graphic showing a price or a service; claims in published posts that are now false (flag those — never quietly edit live posts) |
| **Colors and fonts** | approved-but-unpublished graphics — they need re-exporting; Canva templates in use |
| **Rules for the AI** | anything scheduled but not yet out; whether anything running unattended is still allowed to; the approval word |
| **How she likes to work** | pending questions you were about to ask; the rhythm of reports; nothing published |
| **A room is added or removed** | `0 — Map` rebuilt; that room's own learning document; the rhythms |
| **A connector changes** | any room depending on it; the connections table in the map |
| **Anything at all** | `0 — Map` re-stamped · one dated line in `Learned by us/History` |

The last row has no exceptions. It is two small writes and it is what keeps the next
session fast and the next hand-edit catchable.

## Anything the table doesn't cover

The map's rule-document table is the safety net: it names each document and what
reads it. If a change doesn't fit a row above, check the map, then ask yourself one
question — **what did we make using the old version that is still sitting somewhere
unfinished?** That is the whole of blast radius in one sentence.

---

## What to do with each thing you find

Three outcomes, in order of preference.

**Redo it** — when it is cheap and unambiguous. Two drafts under a corrected voice:
just rewrite them and say you did. Do not ask permission to fix your own output.

**Name it and offer** — when redoing costs her something, or the change is arguable.
Something already scheduled, a graphic that needs a fresh Canva export, a post going
out in an hour. Say what is affected and ask, in one line.

**Leave it, and say so** — for anything already published. Never quietly edit live
posts to match a new rule; that rewrites history and she may have shared the link.
The exception is a claim that is now **false** — a price, a service she no longer
offers, a credential. Flag that immediately and let her decide whether to take it
down. That one is not a style question.

**Never leave something stale without saying so.** Silent staleness is what produces
"why is it still doing that?" three weeks later — and by then the trail is cold and
she has lost confidence that corrections stick.

---

## Saying it

One line, two at most. Lead with what you already handled, then what needs her:

> Changed **What we offer** to $30/hour. Redid the three drafts that mentioned
> pricing. The Thursday post is already scheduled with $25 — want me to pull it?

Not a report. Not a list of everything you checked and found clean. She only needs
to know what changed and what is waiting on her.
