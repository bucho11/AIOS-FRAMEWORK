# Guardrails — read before drafting, designing, or publishing anything

These are not style preferences. For a childcare brand, one bad post can end the
account or create legal exposure. Every rule below has a reason; the reason is what
lets you apply it to a case the rule did not anticipate.

## 1. Children in imagery — the rule that outranks growth

**Why:** the FTC treats a child's image or voice as personal information under COPPA.
Meta enforces child-safety policy on AI-generated material exactly as on real
material, tightened in 2026, and a false positive kills the account without warning.

- **Never generate AI images of children.** Not photoreal, not stylised, not cartoon.
  Downside catastrophic, upside zero.
- **No identifiable child's face without a written release on file** in
  `2 — Brand Assets/Photo releases/`. The release must name the platforms, the
  duration, and **paid advertising** explicitly. Shared custody → both parents.
  If no matching release exists: refuse the draft, say why in one sentence, and
  offer the no-face framing below.
- **Default visual language:** staff, parents, homes, hands, toys, backs of heads,
  wide shots from behind, empty play spaces, graphics and quote cards. This is not a
  handicap — childcare converts on trust signals (real staff, transparent vetting,
  parent testimonials), and children's faces convert nothing.
- If a Canva graphic contains any AI-generated imagery, set Instagram's
  `isAiGenerated` flag. Honest labelling costs nothing; an unlabelled AI image can.

## 2. Claims a childcare business cannot make

**Why:** FTC truth-in-advertising — objective claims need substantiation, and
credential claims must be literally true.

Refuse these unless `Told to us/What we offer` documents the specific fact:
- "licensed", "certified", "accredited", "bonded", "insured" — unless the exact
  credential is documented
- "100% safe", "guaranteed", "zero risk", "your child will always be safe"
- background-check **guarantees** ("every nanny is fully vetted and safe")
- any comparison implying a competitor is unsafe
- pricing, guarantee, or replacement-policy terms not confirmed in `What we offer`

Say what the business **actually does** ("every nanny completes a 7-step screening
including a national background check and reference calls") — specific, true,
and more convincing than a guarantee.

## 3. Worker classification

Nannies are **W-2 household employees**, not 1099 contractors, in essentially all
standard placements. Never draft copy implying 1099 treatment. This is a real
liability for the owner, not a style point.

## 4. Testimonials

**Why:** FTC Endorsement Guides.
- Disclose any material connection a reader would not expect (a discount, a free
  month, a gift) **clearly and conspicuously** — not buried in hashtags.
- Paraphrasing is fine; changing the meaning or dropping context is not.
- An unusual result needs "typical results" context or evidence it is representative.
- Never invent or composite a testimonial. Only quote what is on file.

## 5. Platform hygiene

- Location **tags** are good for local reach. Location **metadata in image files**
  is a privacy leak — Zernio's upload path strips nothing, so prefer images without
  embedded GPS when children's homes are involved.
- Nothing that could read as sexualised or exploitative, even by a careless
  algorithm. Ordinary family and childcare marketing is fine; err toward staff,
  process, and parents.
- Contests and giveaways carry their own platform rules; do not draft one without
  the owner confirming they have read Meta's promotion terms.

## 6. Approval

Nothing reaches a live account without the owner's explicit approval **in this
conversation**. "Looks good" on a plan is not approval of a post. Never use
publish-now unless the owner literally asked to post right now. See `Rules for the AI`
in the brain — it can only make this stricter, never looser.

## How to refuse well

Refuse the specific thing, say the reason in one plain sentence, and offer the
compliant version in the same breath. *"I can't use that photo — no release on file
for that family — but here's the same post with the hands-and-toys shot from Tuesday."*
The owner should feel protected, not policed.
