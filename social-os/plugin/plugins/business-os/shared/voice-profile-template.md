# Voice profile — the template `brand-onboarding` fills into `Told to us/Brand voice`

Every skill that writes in the owner's name reads `Brand voice` first. A correction
the owner makes there holds everywhere, which is the entire point of it being one
file. Rebuilding voice every run wastes her time and makes skills drift apart —
owners notice drift immediately.

Adjectives are useless on their own ("warm and professional" describes every
childcare brand alive). Every dimension below must be a **rule specific enough to
imitate**, and the never-dos matter most: a single "circle back" in a caption is an
instant tell that a machine wrote it.

**This document also outranks the de-slop checks.** Anything here beats a
sounds-human finding: if removing a tell costs her voice, the tell stays and the
reason is said out loud. *Copy that passes every check and says nothing has not been
de-slopped — it has been sanded*, and a voiceless caption is a rewrite where an em
dash was a five-second fix.

```

## How we handle feeling   (the one that matters most)
Name the feeling, don't perform it through the body.

*Why this is a section and not a nicety:* rendering emotion physically is the single
largest gap between human and AI writing in the research — 81% of AI text against 38%
of human. It inverts "show, don't tell", which is exactly the advice caregiver stories
get written under. This brand reaches for it by reflex, which is what makes it the
highest-yield rule in this document.
```

## How to fill it — extraction, not invention

1. Collect **10–20 real posts** she is proud of (or her website + 5 pasted favourites
   if Instagram cannot be read). Golden examples beat volume.
2. For each: score the four sliders, note sentence length, POV, emoji, punctuation,
   CTA form, and any recurring word.
3. Separate **voice invariants** (what never changes) from **tone variables** (what
   shifts by situation). Only invariants go in Mechanics; variables go in the
   situation table.
4. Build the vocabulary bank from words that recur; build **never-use** from words
   that are *absent from every sample* — that absence is the signature.
5. Write do/don't pairs as real rewrites of her own lines, not abstract advice.
6. Play the whole thing back to her in plain English and let her correct it. Her
   edits win completely.
