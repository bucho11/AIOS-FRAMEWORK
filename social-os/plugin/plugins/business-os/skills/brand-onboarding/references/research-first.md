# Research first — what to look at before you say hello

Arrive with a hypothesis. Twenty minutes of reading saves her forty of explaining.

## Her website

Fetch the homepage and the pages it links for services, about, locations, and
"for nannies" / careers. Capture:

- **Services** and how she names them (her words become `What we offer`)
- **Markets / locations** she serves
- **Positioning words** that recur (trust, love, premium, flexible, 24/7…)
- **Credentials she actually states** (screening steps, certifications, insurance) —
  these are the only claims allowed later; write them down exactly
- **CTA she uses** — call, form, "request care", DM
- **Whether there is a nanny-facing page** (recruiting is in scope if so)
- **Colours, fonts, logo** — from the visible design, for `Colors and fonts`
- Any parent testimonials already published (usable, already public)

## Her Instagram

Fetch `https://www.instagram.com/<handle>/`. Instagram often blocks
unauthenticated reads; if so, do not stall — plan to ask for **five favourite
captions** in Phase 4 instead. If it loads, capture:

- Posting frequency and mix (photo / carousel / Reel / graphic)
- Caption length, emoji use, hashtag habit, first-125-character hooks
- Recurring phrases → signature vocabulary
- What the top posts have in common
- Whether children's faces appear (informs how gently to raise the release question)

## Corporate brand material

If she is a franchisee, ask whether corporate provides brand guidelines or approved
content, and whether she may edit it. Read anything she drops into
`2 — Brand Assets/`. Corporate colours and voice usually outrank a local read.

## Turn it into a hypothesis, not a verdict

Open Phase 2 with: *"I read your site and Instagram — it comes across as warm,
family-first, and very focused on trust. Is that right, or is there a side of it I'm
missing?"* Confirmation is fast; correction is informative. Either way she knows
you looked.
